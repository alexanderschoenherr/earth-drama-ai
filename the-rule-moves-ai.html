The Rule Test

Does the rule stay put when the jersey changes?

Bring me a principle and a case. We’ll see what moves.

Political hypocrisy is easy to recognize when the other side commits it. The harder test begins when the person, party, movement, or institution is ours.

The Rule Test helps separate a genuine difference in context from a standard that quietly changes to protect allegiance.

It does not assume two cases are the same. It asks whether the difference in judgment can be explained by a rule that travels with the facts rather than the jersey.

The Principle

Make the rule testable

A slogan can survive almost anything because it is vague enough to move.

The Rule Test begins by turning the slogan into a standard specific enough to bind someone.

The more precisely the rule is stated, the harder it becomes to quietly redefine it around a favored person.

From slogan to rule
“Free speech matters.”
becomes something like: Government should not use official power to punish disfavored political speech merely because it is disfavored.
The governing safeguard

Symmetry is a test.Not a conclusion.

Context matters. Law matters. Scale matters. Evidence matters. Two cases that look similar may deserve different judgments. The question is whether the difference can be explained by a rule that would still make sense if the names disappeared.

The Renaming Test

Did the facts change—or only the name?

Sometimes a violation is not excused. It is renamed. The Rule Test examines whether different language tracks a real factual distinction—or simply the identity of the actor.

The Cost Test

Consistency competes with something

A contradiction is only expensive when consistency is more valuable than what the contradiction protects.

The Rule Test asks what applying the principle to your own side would actually cost.

Not because every inconsistency is secretly psychological—but because principles become hardest to hold precisely when holding them becomes socially, politically, or personally expensive.

PowerWould your side lose an advantage?
BelongingWould you become less aligned with your people?
DignityWould admitting the fact feel humiliating?
CertaintyWould the world become less morally legible?
PrideWould you have to admit an opponent was right?
IdentityCould “we are good” survive “we did something wrong”?

When the Arrow Reverses

Leader→Principle

Political loyalty does not erase real beliefs. But sometimes a leader becomes part of the machinery by which the group decides what the principle itself means. The Test asks which direction the arrow is running in the case you brought.

Possible Verdicts

Diagnosis, not theater

The point is not to force a “gotcha.” Sometimes the most important result is discovering that the cases really are different.

Rule HoldsThe same standard survives materially similar facts.
Context Justifies the DifferenceA relevant factual or legal distinction explains the different judgment.
Legitimate ExceptionThe exception can be stated clearly and generalized beyond the favored side.
Tradeoff, Not HypocrisyThe violation is admitted, but another value is consciously given greater weight.
Standard DriftThe threshold or definition changes in a way that protects allegiance.
RenamingMaterially similar conduct receives different moral language depending on who did it.
Loyalty Over RuleThe allegiance appears to determine what the principle is allowed to mean.
Evidence InsufficientThe facts do not support a confident consistency judgment.
The real test

The Test Comes When the Person Is Yours

Believing in free speech when your people are speaking is easy. Believing in accountability when your enemy is being investigated is easy. Believing in truth when the truth confirms what you already believe is easy.

The harder moment comes when the fact makes your side look bad, applying the rule costs your side power, or admitting what happened costs you pride.

A principle becomes real when it can judge your own side.

Bring The Rule Test a principle and a case. See what moves first: the loyalty—or the rule.

The point is not to become less loyal. It is to find out whether loyalty is deciding what the rule means.
