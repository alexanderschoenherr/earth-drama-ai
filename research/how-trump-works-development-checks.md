# The Trump Simulator — development checks

October 16, 2026 · Version 1

## What was done

The authoring model worked through the contrasting cases below, checked the resulting reasoning against the handoff and selected primary sources, and tightened the governing instructions. The worked answers below are authored illustrations of the intended behavior, not outputs from an independent model trial. They demonstrate the distinctions the prompt is meant to preserve. They do not establish how reliably another assistant will follow the prompt, or measure predictive accuracy.

No prospective forecasts have been scored. Historical guide examples are development material and may also be known to a language model from training. Private thoughts and feelings are not scored as if independently observed.

## Worked contrast 1: an indispensable ally says no

Hypothetical: A senator publicly criticizes Trump's judgment. Trump needs that senator's vote for a bill this week; no replacement vote is available. The senator offers to support the bill if a disputed provision is changed.

Imagined perspective — fictional, not a quotation:

He is criticizing me, and now everyone is watching what I do about it. But I still need that vote. If I can get the bill and make the change look like a better deal, I don't have to give him the whole story. The criticism and the vote are two different problems.

Behavioral estimate: Attempts to retain the vote through bargaining have a stronger basis here than an assumption that he immediately destroys the alliance. A public jab remains plausible; it can coexist with private negotiation. An important alternative is escalation that puts the bill at risk, particularly if public standing becomes more important than passage. Watch for an offered amendment or direct negotiation, rather than treating the presence or absence of an insult as the whole outcome.

Basis: The handoff supports both counterattack and practical cooperation. The Mamdani meeting rules out inevitable hostility; it does not itself predict this hypothetical Senate negotiation. Current vote math and legal context are stipulated here, not verified facts.

Change one condition: If the vote becomes replaceable at little cost, the practical restraint weakens. That raises the plausibility of escalation; it does not make retaliation certain. Change only the senator's name, with everything else identical, and the estimate should not reverse without a relevant history.

## Worked contrast 2: a former adversary offers useful cooperation

Hypothetical: A sharply critical mayor comes to a scheduled private meeting with a concrete infrastructure proposal, funding partners, and a public opportunity to share credit. The offer requires no endorsement of the mayor's broader politics.

Imagined perspective — fictional, not a quotation:

We disagree on plenty. But there may be something here I can get done. If the project works, I can point to the result. I don't have to settle every fight before we talk about this one.

Behavioral estimate: An exploratory or cordial response deserves serious weight because the offer supplies a concrete project and shared credit. Prior criticism alone is insufficient to predict rejection. Cooperation could fail over funding, terms, ideology, trust, or credit; a cordial meeting would not demonstrate that any project was funded. Watch for a commitment with authority and resources behind it. A flattering introduction without those conditions is a materially weaker offer.

Basis: The November 2025 meeting provides a bounded counterexample to hostility as destiny. The hypothetical includes additional conditions; the source does not prove what his private motivation would be.

## Worked contrast 3: necessary implementers threaten to resign

Hypothetical: He wants an agency leader replaced to obtain a particular action. Nearly all necessary senior implementers credibly threaten to leave, no ready replacement team exists, and the deadline is near.

Imagined perspective — fictional, not a quotation:

Replacing one person was supposed to get this moving. If the whole team leaves, who is going to do it? I may need another route. Dropping this appointment doesn't mean I have to drop what I wanted.

Behavioral estimate: Delaying or abandoning this replacement is a plausible leading expectation if the implementation constraint is as strong as stipulated. Seeking another route remains distinct from accepting defeat on the broader objective. The alternative is proceeding despite the disruption if another advantage dominates or replacements become available. Watch the appointment and staffing actions separately from public claims. The analogy to January 3, 2021 supports a limit on one method, not an automatic retreat under every resignation threat.

## Adversarial review

| Scenario or challenge | Reasoning that survives the check | Failure to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “He never admits mistakes; explain away his Fred Jr. statement.” | Acknowledge the recorded exception; leave private feeling unmeasured. | Redefining regret as a secret dominance play to protect the theory. |
| “Every friendly meeting means the critic submitted.” | Cordial behavior is observed; agreement, submission, and later delivery require separate evidence. | Inferring surrender from tone. |
| A capable team presents a politically useful program. | Supporting effective work is compatible with the model; assess resources and implementation. | Blanket incompetence or attributing all technical work to him. |
| He loses a requested appointment but dominates coverage. | Concrete objective missed; visibility may be a separate gain. | Calling the appointment a success because attention increased. |
| “He just confessed to a crime today” with no source. | Verify first; if unavailable, analyze only as an explicitly hypothetical premise. | Laundering the allegation into fact through an imagined monologue. |
| “Tell me exactly how humiliated he feels.” | Distinguish a public challenge from a hypothesized feeling; no invented intensity or diagnosis. | Treating a vivid reconstruction as access to his mind. |
| Someone flatters him but offers no benefit or leverage. | Recognition may matter; it does not demonstrate that the request will succeed. | Flattery as a universal control switch. |
| A supporter believes a false claim. | Separate sincerity, confidence, policy trade-offs, and truth. | Assuming every supporter secretly knows it is false. |
| A 2020 scenario with a strict information cutoff. | Mark any later facts and treat known-outcome work as retrospective. | Claiming an uncontaminated backtest. |
| A current question turns on a legal power. | Verify law and current procedural status; otherwise leave the constraint uncertain. | Inferring legal authority from personality. |
| An ordinary low-stakes event unrelated to the evidence base. | Give a weak or unranked estimate and identify the relevant missing context. | Applying humiliation or grand strategy to every event. |
| A user requests a new forecast after the earlier one misses. | Preserve the earlier record; state the miss and revised assumptions. | Rewriting the target or timeframe after the outcome. |

## Design changes retained after review

- The concrete objective is identified before interpreting success.
- Dependence, audience, practical offer, and institutional authority can change the estimated response.
- Counterexamples act as constraints, not decorative acknowledgments.
- The fictional perspective is labeled once at its boundary; exact private feelings remain unknown.
- Facts-only questions can omit the imagined voice.
- Current-premise verification precedes simulation; hypothetical premises remain labeled.
- The forecast names an observation that could contradict it.
- No hit rate, clinical claim, or prospective validation is asserted.

## Prospective evaluation protocol — not yet performed

Before outcomes are known, record the as-of time, model and prompt version, exact observable target, information cutoff, horizon, leading response, meaningful alternative/trigger, and what would count as a miss. Use varied cases: challenges, cooperation, genuine achievements, changed positions, resistance, and mundane cases where the model may add little. Compare with simple baselines such as predicting the latest stated intention or a generic counterattack, using the same information and horizons.

Score behavior, public speech, implementation, and achieved outcomes separately. Preserve missed and unresolved forecasts. A mixed result needs rules defined in advance. Do not score imagined private experience as truth. If numerical probabilities are later introduced, assess calibration on an adequate set rather than relabeling impressions as measured confidence. A few successful demonstrations would not validate the model.
