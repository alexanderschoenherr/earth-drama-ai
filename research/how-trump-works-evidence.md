# The Trump Simulator: evidence and limits

Version 1 · October 16, 2026

## Provenance and status

Developed from Alexander Schoenherr's supplied research handoff, which identifies How_Trump_Works_Manual.md version 9, Trump_Manual_Notebook_Assessment_and_Sources.md version 4, the published-specialist comparison, and Donald_Trump_Explained.md version 1. Those original files were not independently opened in this development pass. The supplied complete guide is preserved as the reading copy; bracketed citation markers are omitted, and the inherited reference scheme is retained below.

This is an editorial, evidence-informed model. It has no measured predictive accuracy. Primary-source spot-checks below strengthen specific anchors, not every sentence or causal interpretation in the guide. Document dates describe past episodes, not a continuously refreshed account of current politics. All new factual scenarios need their own verification.

## Working model

Keep concrete advantage, public standing/account, and necessary cooperation distinct. Identify the target before assessing success. An announcement, a concession, implementation, and an achieved outcome are different events. A useful reaction need not have been planned. An unsuccessful concrete demand does not become a success simply because it attracts attention.

Public challenge can invite counterattack; the cost of losing necessary cooperation can also invite restraint. A fact dispute can become a loyalty dispute without establishing private belief. A changed offer can change an alliance. Durable policy views, status, political incentives, and coalition goals can coexist. Followers and officials remain actors with purposes and leverage, not extensions of one person's psychology. Performance and delivery require separate evidence.

## Checked anchors

### A. Collective resistance and a limited retreat

The January 6 committee's chapter 4 recounts the January 3, 2021 confrontation over replacing Jeffrey Rosen with Jeffrey Clark, including threatened resignations and concerns about the department's ability to function. It supports an example of necessary cooperation constraining one proposed appointment. It does not show abandonment of the wider election challenge or establish an invariant response to resistance. This is an investigative account grounded in testimony and records, not a criminal verdict.

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-J6-REPORT/html-submitted/ch4.html

### B. Independent duty and personal cooperation

Chapter 5 records pressure on Pence over the electoral-vote count and examines the legal role involved. Use it to distinguish a demanded act from an official's independent obligations. It does not establish that every future official faces identical law, incentives, or choices.

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-J6-REPORT/html-submitted/ch5.html

### C. Cordiality with a political adversary

The November 21, 2025 meeting transcript records Trump and mayor-elect Mamdani discussing affordability and New York while retaining differences. It rules out assuming inevitable hostility from prior criticism alone. It does not establish a durable alliance, the cause of every reaction, or private submission.

https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-during-meeting-with-mayor-elect-zohran-k-mamdani-new-york-city-and-exchange-with

### D. Acknowledgment of error

In a 2019 interview, Trump expressed regret about pressuring Fred Jr. This challenges “never admits mistakes”; it does not independently measure a private feeling. The accessible original interview excerpt was checked, not the entire paywalled article.

https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/trump-pressured-his-alcoholic-brother-about-his-career-now-he-has-regrets-/2019/08/07/58ec2d70-b216-11e9-8f6c-7828e68cb15f_story.html

### E. Threat, agreement, and revocation

The March 14 and March 21, 2025 Paul Weiss orders document restrictions and subsequent revocation, with the latter describing a $40 million pro bono commitment. They establish what the administration ordered and announced, not independent truth of its accusations. A services pledge is not cash paid to Trump or proof of completed services. The guide's account of client-loss concerns is attributed to the firm's chairman via reporting, not independently audited here.

https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/addressing-risks-from-paul-weiss/
https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/addressing-remedial-action-by-paul-weiss/

### F. Capable cooperation and substantive achievement

GAO's February 11, 2021 report describes federal and company efforts to accelerate vaccine development and address manufacturing challenges. It supports recognizing an administrative achievement while keeping agencies, scientists, and companies in the causal account. It does not isolate presidential technical expertise or validate all surrounding COVID statements.

https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-21-319

The Bureau of Prisons explains the First Step Act, signed December 21, 2018, and its bipartisan origins and federal provisions. It constrains blanket no-achievement accounts. It does not imply universal sentencing relief.

https://www.bop.gov/inmates/fsa/overview.jsp

### G. Institutional obligation outside government

The New York attorney general's December 10, 2019 announcement records the court-ordered $2 million Foundation payment. Use the linked resolution and admissions for legal detail. The payment establishes neither a diagnosis nor a universal theory of motive.

https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2019/donald-j-trump-pays-court-ordered-2-million-illegally-using-trump-foundation

### H. Support does not require admiration of conduct

Pew's July 11, 2024 findings distinguish issue agreement from approval of conduct among candidate supporters. In that sample, 88% of Trump's supporters agreed with him on many or all issues; 30% liked his conduct. These dated survey responses do not identify each voter's motive or describe the current coalition.

https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/07/11/biden-and-trumps-personal-qualities-and-handling-of-issues/

### I. Sincere belief and uncertain suspicion

Matthew Graham and Omer Yair's study of election-fraud responses supports taking sincere endorsement and suspicion seriously rather than treating every response as cynical signaling. The author's manuscript abstract and selected passages were checked, and the publication listing confirms the 2024 Political Behavior article. This does not establish Trump's private beliefs or make the fraud claim true.

https://m-graham.com/papers/GrahamYair_BigLie.pdf
https://m-graham.com/research.html

### J. A bounded account of perceived authenticity

Hahl, Kim, and Zuckerman Sivan's publisher abstract describes two vignette experiments supporting a theory of why norm violation can signal an authentic champion under perceived political illegitimacy. It is not a census of Trump supporters' motives. Only the abstract was checked here; the full article and underlying analyses were not independently audited.

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0003122417749632

## Specialist interpretations inherited from the handoff

McAdams's public-source personality interpretations can organize observations; they are not a clinical examination or proof of emotional emptiness. The later PMC article could not be reopened through the research tool during this pass. Blair, Haberman, and Waterhouse illuminate formation and business history; childhood does not explain every adult choice, and complete books were not read here. Haslam, Reicher, and colleagues draw attention to active shared identification; their retrospective January 6 account cannot represent all voters. Graham and Svolik examine partisan/policy trade-offs and punishment of democratic violations, not moral equivalence of all parties. Levitsky and Way direct attention to institutional costs of opposition; their 2025 forecasts do not settle later outcomes. Historical classifications of fascism remain disputed. Weber's distinctions sharpen interpretation but are not his empirical findings about Trump.

No separate result establishes that all supporters knowingly tolerate lies, stay because admitting error is too costly, or change private moral standards at his command. Philosophical reflections about self-laughter or being the center of reality are not diagnostic evidence. Neither “everything is strategy” nor “everything is impulse” is supported. Flattery is not a demonstrated universal control switch.

## Remaining evaluation work

No prospective trial or numerical calibration has been performed. The development checks illustrate failure modes; they do not establish forecast accuracy. Future evaluation needs pre-recorded observable predictions and horizons, explicit scoring rules, comparison with simpler baselines, meaningful negative cases, and unrevised records of misses. Feelings generally have no independent ground truth for scoring. Historical knowledge in training can contaminate retrospective exercises even if outcomes are withheld in the scenario.
