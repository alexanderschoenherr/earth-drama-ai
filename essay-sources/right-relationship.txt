Right Relationship

Why love needs power, courage needs truth, and good things go bad when they stand alone

Human qualities do not become good simply by becoming stronger.

Power can protect or dominate.

Courage can defend the truth or defend a lie.

Intelligence can solve a problem or make us more ingenious at creating one.

Compassion can heal, but without discernment it can also enable.

Freedom can liberate, but without responsibility it can become destructive.

The problem is not always that we have too little of something good.

Sometimes the problem is that a good thing has been separated from the other things that give it its right place.

This is the idea of right relationship.

Two Directions of Development

Human development seems to move in at least two directions.

The first is horizontal.

Horizontal development expands our ability to understand, organize, navigate, and change the world around us.

It includes knowledge, skill, science, technology, infrastructure, wealth, productive capacity, influence, and reach.

It asks:

What can we do?

Can we cross the ocean?

Cure the disease?

Build the company?

Split the atom?

Reach a billion people?

Create a machine that can reason, write, persuade, or invent?

The horizontal expands our possibilities.

The second direction is vertical.

Vertical development concerns the quality of the human being encountering those possibilities.

It includes self-knowledge, discernment, emotional maturity, character, presence, wisdom, courage, conscience, and love.

It asks:

What should we do?

What is worth wanting?

What is true?

What is this power for?

What are we becoming while we use it?

The horizontal expands our options.

The vertical improves our choices.

The horizontal develops the tool.

The vertical develops the user.

The horizontal changes the world.

The vertical changes the one changing it.

Neither is enough by itself.

Love and Power

Love and power may be the clearest example.

Power is the ability to make something happen.

Love helps determine what should happen and whom our power should serve.

Power without love can become domination.

But love without power can remain an intention with no ability to protect, build, intervene, or change material conditions.

The answer is not to reject power in favor of love.

It is to put them into right relationship.

Love gives power direction.
Power gives love consequence.

The same pattern appears elsewhere.

Wisdom / Capability

Wisdom helps determine what capability should serve.

Capability allows wisdom to become effective.

Judgment / Intelligence

Intelligence generates possibilities.

Judgment determines which possibilities deserve pursuit.

Ethics / Technology

Technology expands what can be done.

Ethics helps determine what should be done.

Character / Talent

Talent enlarges what a person can accomplish.

Character helps determine what that ability becomes in the world.

Purpose / Productivity

Productivity increases output.

Purpose determines what is worth producing.

The vertical does not exist merely to restrain the horizontal.

It orients it.

And the horizontal is not merely a temptation that the vertical must resist.

It gives inner understanding the ability to become outward reality.

Depth must become capable.
Capability must become conscious.

Right Relationship Is Larger Than Two Axes

Not every important pairing is a horizontal quality matched with a vertical one.

Right relationship is the larger principle.

There are at least three forms it can take.

1. Orientation and Capability

These are the clearest vertical–horizontal relationships.

Love and power.

Wisdom and capability.

Judgment and intelligence.

Ethics and technology.

Humanity and efficiency.

Conscience and authority.

Stewardship and growth.

One side enlarges what can be done.

The other helps determine what that ability should serve.

They answer different questions:

Can we?

and

Should we?

How?

and

Toward what?

The mature relationship requires both.

2. Qualities That Correct One Another

Other pairings exist largely within the development of character itself.

Consider truth and courage.

Truth without courage may remain unspoken.

Courage without truth can become recklessness or bravery in the service of something false.

So:

Truth tells courage where to stand.
Courage gives truth a life in the world.

Or:

In the absence of courage, truth is an orphan.

The same pattern appears elsewhere.

Honesty / Compassion

Honesty without compassion can become cruelty.

Compassion without honesty can become concealment.

Confidence / Humility

Confidence without humility can become arrogance.

Humility without confidence can become self-erasure.

Conviction / Curiosity

Conviction without curiosity can harden into dogma.

Curiosity without conviction can leave us unable to commit.

Love / Truth

Love without truth can become enabling.

Truth without love can become a weapon.

Compassion / Discernment

Compassion without discernment can lose its boundaries.

Discernment without compassion can become coldness.

These qualities do more than limit each other.

They calibrate each other.

Each helps the other find its mature form.

3. Living Polarities

Some relationships contain two legitimate needs that can never be permanently resolved in favor of one side.

Freedom / Responsibility

Freedom without responsibility can become license.

Responsibility without freedom can become obedience.

Autonomy / Belonging

Autonomy without belonging can become isolation.

Belonging without autonomy can become conformity.

Order / Liberty

Order without liberty can become domination.

Liberty without enough order can become instability.

Tradition / Innovation

Tradition without innovation can become stagnation.

Innovation without memory can become amnesia.

Stability / Change

Too much stability and a system cannot adapt.

Too much change and nothing can endure.

Action / Reflection

Action without reflection repeats mistakes.

Reflection without action never enters the world.

These are not problems that can be solved by declaring one side good and the other bad.

Nor does right relationship mean dividing everything fifty-fifty.

Sometimes courage is needed more than caution.

Sometimes caution is wisdom.

Sometimes mercy should prevail.

Sometimes justice requires a harder boundary.

Sometimes something should be preserved.

Sometimes it should be destroyed so that something better can take its place.

The question is not:

Which side wins?

It is:

What belongs here, now, in what proportion, and in service of what?

That is part of what wisdom is.

When Good Things Lose Their Place

This leads to a deeper possibility.

Some destructive forces begin not with the complete absence of something good, but with a real human capacity that has escaped the relationships that once governed it.

Power is not inherently evil.

Courage is not automatically virtuous.

Intelligence is not wisdom.

Compassion is not discernment.

Freedom is not maturity.

Ambition is not meaning.

Technology is not progress.

Separated from the qualities that orient them, even valuable capacities can become distorted.

Power without love can become domination.

Courage without wisdom can become fanaticism.

Truth without compassion can become cruelty.

Compassion without boundaries can become self-erasure.

Intelligence without judgment can become sophisticated stupidity.

Freedom without responsibility can become license.

Order without liberty can become authoritarianism.

Ambition without meaning can become endless acquisition.

Technology without ethics can become capability without conscience.

Spirituality without reality-testing can become fantasy or delusion.

Reason without humility can become false certainty.

Faith without discernment can become credulity.

Community without individuality can become conformity.

Individuality without community can become alienation.

The failure is not always excess.

Sometimes it is disconnection.

Something has lost its proper relationship to the whole.

The Same Problem Appears in Civilization

Individuals are not the only things that can develop unevenly.

Civilizations can too.

Humanity has become extraordinarily good at horizontal development.

We learned to manipulate matter.

Harness energy.

Cross oceans.

Transform landscapes.

Organize millions of strangers.

Build enormous stores of wealth and knowledge.

Communicate instantly across the planet.

Give nearly anyone the power to publish.

And now we are building machines capable of generating language, images, code, analysis, persuasion, and invention.

These are extraordinary achievements.

But greater capability does not automatically produce better judgment.

Knowing how to split the atom does not tell us what to do once the atom has been split.

Connecting billions of people does not teach them how to relate to one another.

Giving everyone a publishing platform does not give everyone the wisdom to know what deserves amplification.

Artificial intelligence can expand the range of things human beings are able to do.

Capability alone cannot tell us what kind of civilization those abilities should serve.

This is the imbalance:

Our power can develop faster than our ability to govern power.

The answer is not to stop developing horizontally.

We still need more knowledge.

More science.

Better technology.

Greater capability.

More effective institutions.

More abundance.

But those powers increasingly require human beings capable of using them without being completely governed by fear, greed, resentment, tribalism, status, compulsion, vanity, or the intoxication of power itself.

We have spent centuries asking how much more powerful humanity can become.

The growing question is:

What kind of human being is becoming so powerful?

The Principle

Right relationship is not the same thing as moderation.

It does not mean every quality should be reduced toward the middle.

And it does not mean everything should be balanced equally.

It means that our powers, virtues, desires, capacities, and values must exist in relationships that allow each to find its proper form.

Love does not weaken power.

It makes power worthy.

Power does not corrupt love simply by existing.

It can make love effective.

Wisdom does not make capability smaller.

It makes capability intelligent.

Capability does not cheapen wisdom.

It gives wisdom consequence.

Courage does not create truth.

It allows truth to enter the world.

Truth does not diminish courage.

It gives courage something worth risking for.

Each gives the other its right place.

Each protects the other from a characteristic distortion.

And each can allow the other to become more fully what it was capable of being.

Perhaps that is one of the deeper patterns beneath human flourishing:

We do not become whole by maximizing every good quality independently.

We become whole by putting our powers into right relationship with one another.

Civilization may face the same task.

For centuries, humanity learned how to become more capable.

Now our capability is becoming powerful enough that another kind of development can no longer remain optional.

The horizontal changes what becomes possible.
The vertical determines what possibility becomes.

The task is not to choose depth over power.

It is to unite them.

To make wisdom effective.
And power wise.
