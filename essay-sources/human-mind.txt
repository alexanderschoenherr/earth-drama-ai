HUMAN MIND™

User Guide

For human use only.
Please read before operating.

⸻

1. Before You Begin

You have a mind.

It is already running.

It has, in fact, been running for quite some time.

You may have been using it for years without knowing exactly what it is, what it does, which functions happen automatically, which functions can be influenced, or why it occasionally wakes you up at 3:14 A.M. because apparently 1998 requires further investigation.

This is normal.

The Human Mind is used for thinking, remembering, imagining, interpreting, learning, deciding, creating, worrying, loving, regretting, anticipating, believing, doubting, paying attention, losing attention, finding meaning, inventing meaning, and having imaginary conversations with people who are not currently present.

Many operations begin automatically.

You do not manually request every thought.

You do not select every memory before it appears.

You do not approve every emotion before feeling it.

You do not choose every place your attention goes.

IMPORTANT: Automatic activation does not necessarily indicate user intent.

Something occurring in the mind does not necessarily mean you deliberately put it there.

Something occurring in the mind does not necessarily mean you must do anything about it.

IMPORTANT: Output is not necessarily instruction.

The first thing to learn about your mind is not how to control it.

The first thing to learn is that you can notice what it is doing.

2. Basic Components

Awareness / Observation
Notices mental activity.
Location undetermined.

Attention
Selects what enters the foreground.
Limited capacity.

Perception
Builds an experience from incoming information.
Input may be incomplete.

Thought Generation
Produces ideas, judgments, questions, images, plans, nonsense, and other internal material.
Often spontaneous.

Interpretation
Assigns meaning to information.
Multiple interpretations possible.

Memory
Makes past information available in the present.
Incomplete and changeable.

Imagination / Simulation
Generates things that are not currently happening.
Not a prediction device.

Emotion
Produces feeling states.
Signal does not automatically equal command.

Desire / Motivation
Generates movement toward or away from possible outcomes.
Wanting does not guarantee wisdom.

Evaluation / Values
Helps determine what matters and what is worth doing.
May disagree with desire.

Belief
Maintains assumptions about what is true.
May be updated.

Identity
Maintains a working model of the user.
Model may be mistaken for user.

Language
Uses symbols to organize thought and transmit meaning.
Works internally and between users.

Learning / Habit
Adapts through experience and repetition.
Repeated use trains the device.

3. Inputs, Perception, and Interpretation

The Human Mind is constantly receiving input.

Information arrives from sight, sound, touch, smell, taste, the body, memory, language, other people, and the environment.

You do not experience all available information.

The system selects, organizes, compares, predicts, and fills gaps.

This is useful when identifying a chair in dim light.

It can be less useful when identifying what someone “really meant” by saying:

Sure.

The mind does not simply receive an event untouched.

It asks:

What does this mean?

Example

EVENT: Someone has not answered your text.

Possible interpretations:

* They’re busy.
* They forgot.
* They’re asleep.
* They’re angry.
* They’re losing interest.
* I said something wrong.
* They hate me.
* This relationship is over.
* Nobody has ever loved me correctly.

The external event has not changed.

The internal world has.

Interpretation is an operation.

What happened and what your mind makes of what happened are not always exactly the same thing.

To inspect an interpretation

1. Identify what actually happened.
2. Identify what your mind concluded it meant.
3. Ask whether another explanation could also fit.

You do not have to choose the nicest explanation.

Just notice that an explanation was added.

4. Automatic Operation

Many functions operate without waiting for instruction.

A thought appears.

Attention moves toward it.

An interpretation forms.

An emotion follows.

An urge arises.

Sometimes this happens extremely quickly.

Example

INPUT: Someone uses a period instead of an exclamation point.

AUTOMATIC PROCESSING:

Something feels different.

Are they upset?

They must be upset.

What did I do?

I knew this was happening.

Should I ask?

No. Asking will make me look insane.

Maybe they want me to ask.

Actually, this is manipulative.

You have now spent twenty-seven minutes investigating punctuation.

The system is functioning.

Possibly too enthusiastically, but functioning.

Remember:

A thought appearing is not the same thing as choosing the thought.

A feeling appearing is not the same thing as choosing the feeling.

An impulse appearing is not the same thing as choosing the action.

A frightening possibility appearing is not the same thing as receiving information from the future.

5. Observation Mode

A strange capability may eventually become apparent.

You can notice a thought.

A thought appears:

Everybody hates me.

Then:

I am noticing the thought that everybody hates me.

The thought may still be there.

You have simply seen it as a thought.

The same thing can happen with emotion:

I am angry.

Then:

I am noticing that I am angry.

Or attention:

I need to solve this right now.

Then:

I have been thinking about this for forty-five minutes.

The mind does things.

You can notice it doing them.

NOTE: Observation does not guarantee control. It creates options.

Basic operating procedure

1. Notice that something is happening.
2. Name it if you can: thought, fear, memory, interpretation, desire, impulse.
3. Do not immediately assume it must be fixed or obeyed.
4. Decide what, if anything, to do next.

The procedure is simple.

Remembering to use it is the difficult part.

6. Understanding Output

Output — Important note
Thought — Something the mind produced. Not automatically fact.
Emotion — A real experience. Not automatically a verdict.
Memory — Past information appearing now.
Imagination — Internally generated simulation. Not prediction.
Interpretation — Meaning assigned to information.
Desire — A movement toward or away. Not automatically instruction.
Belief — An assumption held to be true. May be outdated.
Identity — The mind’s current model of the user.

The Human Mind produces both excellent thoughts and complete garbage.

No indicator light is provided.

7. Attention

At any moment, more information is available than you can fully attend to.

Sometimes you direct attention.

Sometimes something captures it.

Threat is good at this.

So are novelty, desire, repetition, outrage, notifications, unresolved questions, sex, fear, and people you would supposedly like to stop thinking about.

What has your attention is not necessarily what deserves your attention.

To redirect attention

1. Notice where it is.
2. Choose where you want it instead.
3. Put it there.
4. When it wanders, return it.

It may wander again.

Return it again.

Attention is not a one-time command.

8. Desire, Motivation, and Values

The Human Mind does not merely notice things.

It also wants.

It generates:

move toward this

and

move away from this.

Common targets include food, sex, safety, pleasure, relief, approval, status, belonging, achievement, love, certainty, and rest.

Desire can help build a life.

It can also persuade you that something you desperately want must therefore be good for you.

These are different claims.

DESIRE: What do I want?

AVERSION: What do I want to avoid?

VALUE JUDGMENT: What is actually worth doing?

The answers may disagree.

You can want something and decide not to pursue it.

You can fear something and decide to face it.

The system is capable of wanting things that harm it and resisting things that would help it.

No contradiction is detected.

To evaluate a desire

Ask:

1. What do I want?
2. What do I expect it to do for me?
3. What will probably happen if I act on it?
4. Is that consistent with what matters to me?

Desire supplies energy.

It does not have to supply policy.

9. Memory

The Human Mind allows events that no longer exist to affect the present.

This ability is required for learning.

It is also how something that happened fifteen years ago can ruin Thursday afternoon.

A memory is happening now.

The event it concerns happened before.

The event may be over while your relationship to it is still active.

The past event is fixed.

Your present relationship to it is not always fixed.

NOTE: Peace with the past does not require approval of the past.

When a memory is governing the present

1. Identify what is being remembered.
2. Confirm whether it is happening now.
3. Notice what meaning the memory currently carries.
4. Ask whether that meaning is still the only one available.

Do not attempt to change the past.

The editable part is your present relationship to it.

⸻

10. Imagination / Simulation

The Human Mind can generate something internally that is not presently happening.

This allows the user to plan, invent, rehearse, fantasize, empathize, create art, and imagine a different future.

It also allows the user to become emotionally devastated by something that may happen on Tuesday.

Same system.

Different application.

The simulator occasionally forgets to label the simulation.

A hypothetical event can produce real fear.

An imaginary argument can produce real anger.

A fantasy can produce real desire.

A possibility can begin to feel like a prediction.

The simulator is powerful.

Use accordingly.

11. Installed Settings

Not everything currently operating inside your mind was deliberately selected by you.

Settings may have been influenced by parents, family, school, religion, culture, friends, enemies, lovers, advertising, the internet, repeated experience, humiliation, success, and things someone said to you once and apparently had administrative privileges.

Some settings were installed before the user was old enough to review permissions.

Common examples:

People cannot be trusted.

I always fail.

Nobody stays.

This is just who I am.

Old settings can affect what the system notices, expects, attempts, and interprets.

Age does not make a setting a permanent specification.

12. Language and Learning

The Human Mind can convert internal experience into symbols.

Words let the system name things, organize ideas, ask questions, remember instructions, make distinctions, tell stories, and communicate with other users.

Language also allows the mind to communicate with itself.

Compare:

I am scared.

I am noticing fear.

I am scared, therefore something terrible is happening.

These are not the same operation.

Language does not magically change reality.

It participates in how the mind represents reality.

Use precise language where precision matters.

Especially internally.

The Human Mind can also change through learning.

Repeated practice is one way this happens.

Something difficult becomes familiar.

Something familiar becomes easier.

Something repeated often enough may happen with very little deliberate effort.

This is how humans learn to read, drive, play instruments, perform routines, and check their phones every forty seconds.

The system does not always distinguish between:

This is useful

and

This keeps happening.

Repeated use trains the device.

Choose repetitions with some care.

13. What You Can Do With It

Once basic operation is understood, the Human Mind can be used deliberately.

Focus

Choose something and return attention to it when the device wanders.

Ask Questions

Why? What happened? What am I missing? What else could be true?

Questions change what the system searches for.

Use good ones.

Reason

Compare information. Notice contradictions. Test explanations. Separate what you know from what you assume.

Change your mind when better information arrives.

This feature is optional but strongly recommended.

Imagine

Create something internally before it exists externally.

A song. A solution. A room. A future. A different way of living.

Rehearse

Practice before the body acts.

A speech. A difficult conversation. A performance. A response.

Reconsider

Inspect an interpretation. Question a belief. Reopen a conclusion.

The first explanation generated by the device is not always the final one.

Learn

Become capable of something you cannot currently do.

Create

Combine existing material into something that did not exist before.

Words become stories.

Sounds become songs.

Ideas become objects.

An invisible event inside one person can eventually become part of the physical world.

14. Choice and External Output

Mental activity eventually reaches the world through behavior.

The system may generate several thoughts, emotions, desires, urges, and possible actions.

The user does not have to perform all of them.

THOUGHT: Tell him exactly what you think.

ALTERNATE THOUGHT: Never speak to him again.

EMOTION: Rage.

IMAGINATION: Eight-minute speech.

DESIRE: Immediate relief.

VALUE: Do not make tomorrow worse.

AVAILABLE ACTION: Wait until tomorrow.

Choice is the point where internal possibility becomes external consequence.

To choose deliberately

1. Notice the available actions.
2. Ask what each is likely to produce.
3. Ask which result is most consistent with what matters to you.
4. Act.
5. Observe what happens.
6. Update accordingly.

This does not guarantee a good decision.

It is simply better than skipping steps 1 through 3.

IMPORTANT: You may not choose every thought. You have considerably more responsibility for which thoughts become actions.

15. Multi-User Operation

Human Minds can connect to other Human Minds.

This greatly expands available capability.

Communication

A thought exists privately inside one mind.

The user makes sounds with the mouth.

Another user hears them.

Some version of the thought may now exist inside the other mind.

Human beings became so accustomed to this feature that they stopped noticing how strange it is.

Writing allows the same transfer across distance and time.

A person can die.

Centuries later, something they thought can still appear inside yours.

WARNING: Transmission is lossy.

What one user means and what another receives are not guaranteed to be identical.

⸻

Learning From Other Users

Another user can spend twenty years learning something and transfer part of the result to you in twenty minutes.

You do not need to personally rediscover fire, agriculture, algebra, antibiotics, harmony, or how to make bread.

Someone already worked on these.

Use available documentation.

⸻

Collaboration

Other minds contain information yours does not.

Different users have different experiences, knowledge, abilities, blind spots, and ways of seeing.

Several minds working together can produce something no individual user would have produced alone.

One notices something.

Another develops it.

Another challenges it.

Another knows how to build it.

Another makes it beautiful.

Another realizes the entire idea is terrible.

This can save considerable time.

⸻

Coordination

Multiple Human Minds can hold the same imagined thing and organize behavior around it.

A plan.

A company.

A law.

A currency.

A movie that has not been made yet.

A meeting next Thursday at 2:00.

Once enough users share a representation, it can begin producing physical consequences in the world.

One human cannot build a city.

Connected humans can.

A network of minds can become capable of actions unavailable to any individual unit.

This is one of the primary reasons humans are dangerous.

It is also one of the primary reasons humans are magnificent.

IMPORTANT: Increased network power should not be confused with improved judgment.

16. Security and Unauthorized Influence

The Human Mind is an influenceable system.

Other users, institutions, technologies, and environments may attempt to direct your attention, emotions, beliefs, desires, and behavior.

Common methods include repetition, fear, outrage, flattery, authority, shame, status, urgency, reward, belonging, sexual attraction, selective information, and very confident people with microphones.

Some influence is useful.

Teaching is influence.

Love is influence.

Advice is influence.

Art is influence.

The issue is not whether you are influenced.

You are.

The useful questions are:

By what?

Toward what?

⚠️ FAMILIARITY MAY BE MISTAKEN FOR TRUTH.

⚠️ OUTRAGE MAY BE USED TO CAPTURE ATTENTION.

⚠️ SOCIAL CONSENSUS MAY BE MISTAKEN FOR VERIFICATION.

⚠️ URGENCY MAY REDUCE INSPECTION TIME.

⚠️ DESIRE MAY BE EXTERNALLY STIMULATED.

No firewall is currently available that prevents all unwanted influence.

Awareness remains the primary security feature.

18. Troubleshooting

Problem: I cannot stop thinking about something.

Determine whether additional thinking is producing anything new.

If not, the thinking itself may now be the activity.

⸻

Problem: The device keeps replaying an old event.

Confirm that the event is not presently occurring.

The event cannot be changed.

The relationship to the event may be adjustable.

⸻

Problem: I am currently arguing with someone who is not here.

Verify presence of other party.

If absent, note that the conversation is a simulation.

Continue only if useful.

⸻

Problem: I am experiencing an emergency that has not happened yet.

Check whether you are planning or predicting.

Simulation is not prediction.

⸻

Problem: I know something intellectually but do not feel it emotionally.

The intellect may have received the update before the rest of the system.

Allow additional processing time.

⸻

Problem: I keep doing something I consciously do not want to do.

Check for habit.

Check environmental cues.

Check what reward, relief, avoidance, or familiarity the behavior provides.

The system may be executing an old routine.

⸻

Problem: Another user does not understand what I mean.

Do not immediately increase volume.

Check whether the transmitted message resembles the message you intended to send.

Transmission is lossy.

⸻

Problem: Everyone around me believes the same thing.

This may indicate that the belief is true.

It may also indicate that everyone around you talks to one another.

Further inspection recommended.

⸻

Problem: I want something very badly.

Confirm whether this is:

a desire

or

a decision.

They are different functions.

⸻

Problem: Everything suddenly seems terrible.

See Care and Operating Conditions before making major conclusions.

19. Care and Operating Conditions

Performance may vary according to sleep, food, stress, illness, movement, isolation, relationships, substances, physical safety, repeated information, and environment.

If system performance changes, do not immediately conclude that you have discovered a permanent truth about yourself.

Sometimes the machine is tired.

Sometimes it is hungry.

Sometimes it has been reading the internet for six hours.

Maintenance is not enlightenment.

It still matters.
